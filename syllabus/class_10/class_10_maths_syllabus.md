# CBSE Class 10 Mathematics Syllabus 2024-25

## Metadata

- **Class**: 10
- **Subject**: Mathematics

## Overview

The CBSE Class 10 Mathematics syllabus is designed to provide students with a strong foundation in mathematical concepts and problem-solving skills. The curriculum covers Algebra, Geometry, Trigonometry, Statistics, and Probability.

## Unit-wise Syllabus

### Unit I: Number Systems (6 Marks)

#### Chapter 1: Real Numbers
**Topics:**
- Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic - statements after reviewing work done earlier and after illustrating and motivating through examples
- Proofs of irrationality of √2, √3, √5
- Decimal representation of rational numbers in terms of terminating/non-terminating recurring decimals

**Learning Outcomes:**
- Understand Euclid's division lemma
- Apply Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic
- Prove irrationality of numbers
- Determine nature of decimal expansion

### Unit II: Algebra (20 Marks)

#### Chapter 2: Polynomials
**Topics:**
- Zeros of a polynomial
- Relationship between zeros and coefficients of quadratic polynomials
- Statement and simple problems on division algorithm for polynomials with real coefficients

**Key Formulas:**
- Sum of zeros (α + β) = -b/a
- Product of zeros (αβ) = c/a

#### Chapter 3: Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables
**Topics:**
- Pair of linear equations in two variables and graphical method of their solution
- Consistency/inconsistency
- Algebraic conditions for number of solutions
- Solution of a pair of linear equations in two variables algebraically:
  - Substitution method
  - Elimination method
  - Cross-multiplication method
- Simple situational problems

**Types of Solutions:**
- Unique solution (intersecting lines)
- Infinite solutions (coincident lines)
- No solution (parallel lines)

#### Chapter 4: Quadratic Equations
**Topics:**
- Standard form of a quadratic equation: ax² + bx + c = 0, (a ≠ 0)
- Solutions of quadratic equations (only real roots) by:
  - Factorization
  - Completing the square
  - Quadratic formula
- Relationship between discriminant and nature of roots
- Situational problems based on quadratic equations

**Key Concepts:**
- Discriminant (D) = b² - 4ac
- D > 0: Two distinct real roots
- D = 0: Two equal real roots
- D < 0: No real roots

#### Chapter 5: Arithmetic Progressions
**Topics:**
- Motivation for studying Arithmetic Progression
- Derivation of the nth term and sum of the first n terms of an A.P.
- Application in solving daily life problems

**Key Formulas:**
- nth term: aₙ = a + (n-1)d
- Sum of n terms: Sₙ = n/2[2a + (n-1)d] = n/2(a + l)

### Unit III: Coordinate Geometry (6 Marks)

#### Chapter 6: Coordinate Geometry
**Topics:**
- Review: Concepts of coordinate geometry, graphs of linear equations
- Distance formula
- Section formula (internal division)
- Area of a triangle

**Key Formulas:**
- Distance: √[(x₂-x₁)² + (y₂-y₁)²]
- Section formula: ((mx₂+nx₁)/(m+n), (my₂+ny₁)/(m+n))
- Area of triangle: ½|x₁(y₂-y₃) + x₂(y₃-y₁) + x₃(y₁-y₂)|

### Unit IV: Geometry (15 Marks)

#### Chapter 7: Triangles
**Topics:**
- Definitions, examples, counter examples of similar triangles
- (Prove) If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, the other two sides are divided in the same ratio (Basic Proportionality Theorem)
- (Motivate) If a line divides two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, the line is parallel to the third side
- (Motivate) If in two triangles, the corresponding angles are equal, their corresponding sides are proportional and the triangles are similar (AAA)
- (Motivate) If the corresponding sides of two triangles are proportional, their corresponding angles are equal and the triangles are similar (SSS)
- (Motivate) If one angle of a triangle is equal to one angle of another triangle and the sides including these angles are proportional, the two triangles are similar (SAS)
- (Prove) The ratio of the areas of two similar triangles is equal to the ratio of the squares of their corresponding sides
- (Prove) In a right triangle, the square on the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of the squares on the other two sides (Pythagoras Theorem)
- (Prove) In a triangle, if the square on one side is equal to sum of the squares on the other two sides, the angle opposite to the first side is a right angle (Converse of Pythagoras Theorem)

#### Chapter 8: Circles
**Topics:**
- Tangent to a circle at a point of contact
- (Prove) The tangent at any point of a circle is perpendicular to the radius through the point of contact
- (Prove) The lengths of tangents drawn from an external point to a circle are equal

### Unit V: Trigonometry (12 Marks)

#### Chapter 9: Introduction to Trigonometry
**Topics:**
- Trigonometric ratios of an acute angle of a right-angled triangle
- Proof of their existence (well defined)
- Values of trigonometric ratios of 30°, 45°, 60° and 90°
- Relationships between the ratios
- Trigonometric Identities:
  - sin²A + cos²A = 1
  - sec²A - tan²A = 1
  - cosec²A - cot²A = 1

**Trigonometric Ratios Table:**
| Angle | 0° | 30° | 45° | 60° | 90° |
|-------|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| sin | 0 | 1/2 | 1/√2 | √3/2 | 1 |
| cos | 1 | √3/2 | 1/√2 | 1/2 | 0 |
| tan | 0 | 1/√3 | 1 | √3 | ∞ |

#### Chapter 10: Some Applications of Trigonometry
**Topics:**
- Angle of elevation, Angle of depression
- Simple problems on heights and distances
- Problems should not involve more than two right triangles

### Unit VI: Mensuration (10 Marks)

#### Chapter 11: Areas Related to Circles
**Topics:**
- Area of sectors and segments of a circle
- Problems based on areas and perimeter/circumference of plane figures
- Problems on finding area of segment

**Key Formulas:**
- Area of sector = (θ/360°) × πr²
- Length of arc = (θ/360°) × 2πr
- Area of segment = Area of sector - Area of triangle

#### Chapter 12: Surface Areas and Volumes
**Topics:**
- Surface areas and volumes of combinations of:
  - Cubes
  - Cuboids
  - Spheres
  - Hemispheres
  - Right circular cylinders/cones
- Conversion of one solid shape to another

**Key Formulas:**
- Cylinder: V = πr²h, SA = 2πr(r+h)
- Cone: V = (1/3)πr²h, SA = πr(l+r)
- Sphere: V = (4/3)πr³, SA = 4πr²
- Hemisphere: V = (2/3)πr³, CSA = 2πr²

### Unit VII: Statistics and Probability (11 Marks)

#### Chapter 13: Statistics
**Topics:**
- Mean, median and mode of grouped data (bimodal situation to be avoided)
- Mean by Direct method and Assumed mean method only

**Key Formulas:**
- Mean (Direct): x̄ = Σfᵢxᵢ/Σfᵢ
- Mean (Assumed): x̄ = a + Σfᵢdᵢ/Σfᵢ
- Median: l + [(n/2 - cf)/f] × h
- Mode: l + [(f₁ - f₀)/(2f₁ - f₀ - f₂)] × h

#### Chapter 14: Probability
**Topics:**
- Classical definition of probability
- Simple problems on finding the probability of an event
- Complementary events

**Key Concepts:**
- P(E) = Number of favorable outcomes / Total number of outcomes
- P(E) + P(not E) = 1
- 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1

## Important Topics for Board Exam

### High Weightage Topics:
1. **Algebra**
   - Quadratic Equations (roots, discriminant, word problems)
   - Arithmetic Progressions (nth term, sum of n terms)
   - Linear Equations (graphical and algebraic methods)

2. **Geometry**
   - Similar Triangles (BPT, criteria for similarity)
   - Circles (tangent properties, problems)
   - Pythagoras Theorem and its applications

3. **Trigonometry**
   - Trigonometric identities and proofs
   - Heights and Distances problems

4. **Mensuration**
   - Surface areas and volumes of combined solids
   - Areas related to circles

5. **Statistics**
   - Mean, median, mode of grouped data
